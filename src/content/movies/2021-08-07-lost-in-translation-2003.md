This movie really had to make me going and feel emotions, huh? Wamth, fuzziness, happiest, sadness, loneliness, hopefulness, all the things. I sat for a bit, just watching the credits roll, to give myself time to process it.

If nothing else, I really empathised with the feelings of being stuck in an unfamiliar place, all by yourself, without people around you as a network to spend time with and lean on when you need support. <i>Lost in Translation</i> expertly translates that lyrical loneliness into film and I'm equal parts glad and upset I was able to relive it again.

There's a beautiful romance that blossoms between Murray and Johansson's characters, two kindred spirits at very different stages of their lives who find each other in the noise and bright lights of a sleepless city in a foreign country. Their relationship flowers as they share their nights together, not necessarily in a romantic way at first but as a mutual kind of love for each other - two people experiencing the same emotions of loss, loneliness and, above all else, an uncertainty about their place and purpose in the world. This relationship is overshadowed and made all the more fragile by the knowledge that its destined to end, to fail, that within the week they will be forced apart from one another, pulled away from their brief period of respite and thrust back into the lives they so desperately want to escape.

The second half is definitely where this movie's strengths lie, for me, but I think that makes sense given the first half is used very effectively to set everything up. Murray and Johansson both perform amazingly in this, and the atmosphere and emotion throughout is really, really, very good.
