There's something exhilarating about clunky, lo-fi science fiction. The technologies used are so close to current day counterparts that their weaknesses and limitations are still heavily apparent. In these movies the stakes are higher, the losses greater, and every tiny achievement - be it simply landing your drop pod, or retrieving a single rare gem - seems massive in comparison to the dangers you have to place yourself in just to survive. 

<b><i>Prospect</i> captures all of this and maybe more.</b>

I love science fiction, and I haven't been this engaged and interested in a film of this genre since <i>Ad Astra </i>which, looking back at, I would say contains a lot of similarities. 
Pedro Pascal is, unsurprisingly, very good in this, and I hope we get to see Sophie Thatcher in a lot more stuff to come!

If I <i>had</i> to gripe about something, I would probably touch on the "indie-ness" of it a bit; the locations sometimes feel a bit <i>too</i> Earth-like (which is understandably tricky and probably unavoidable when working with a low budget), and it occasionally drew me out of the immersion and made me a bit too aware that I was watching some actors wander around in a Washington State rainforest.

<i><b>But</b></i>, I don't like that I've written almost as many words on my negative thoughts as my positive, because that <i>definitely</i> isn't representative. <i>Prospect</i> is a very good movie and really recommend you watch it.
