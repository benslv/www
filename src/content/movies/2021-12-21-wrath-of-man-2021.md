Guy Ritchie makes films for Real Men who like shouting and swearing and fucking and killing, and they need to be all about men and guns and money and guns, and women aren't allowed to be in it unless they're fridged or fucked or killed—unless it's a masculine woman, then it's okay for her to have one line about a man's dick size—and the dialogue has to solely consist of grunts and swearing and quips and hollow one-liners that don't actually mean anything. They all need to be exactly two hours long and consist of three very well-defined acts culminating in the Good Guy—who isn't really good because he's still a gangster but he's better than the other gangsters because they kill more people and are rude—exacting a good amount of premeditated revenge on the Bad Guy and we have to feel triumphant about that fact, as the movie ends with a slow fade on our Good Guy and the credits roll to some funky, hip-hop music.

And yet, despite saying all this and being aware of all this, I still enjoyed watching <i>Wrath of Man</i>. Nowhere near as much as <i>The Gentlemen</i> or <i>Snatch</i>, which both oozed that quintessential Guy Ritchie flair and style, but I still thought it was fine. The change of pace to a much more serious, straightforward approach was engaging, and I was genuinely intrigued by the plot as bits of it were slowly revealed as the film progressed.
